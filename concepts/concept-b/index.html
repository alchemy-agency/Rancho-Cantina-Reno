Happy hour, Taco Tuesdays, Sunday brunch. Tequila, mezcal, and a river to look at.

The vaquero

Spanish cattle, open range, and the riders who worked it. The first ranch cooking in the West was done over coals, on iron, outdoors.

The ranch table

Family recipes seasoned the Californio way, carried east over the Sierra. Chile, garlic, and salt on whatever the fire could hold.

The buckaroo

Nevada's rancho era, 1860s on. Great Basin ranching, pine nuts and sagebrush, and a diet built around beans, beef, and open fire.

The rancho, today

Reno, on the Truckee. Same fire, same welcome. Rancho by tradition. Reno by nature. Always by heart.

Family owned.
Family cooked.

Rancho Cantina is a family restaurant first. The recipes come from one kitchen and the same people are still standing in it. In Reno you will find them at the pass, on the patio, and probably at the bar.
